The Daily Stoic: 366 Meditations on Wisdom, Perseverance, and the Art of Living

by Ryan Holiday
★★★★★

The Daily Stoic delivers timeless wisdom through short, accessible daily entries, proving that Stoicism isn't about suppressing emotion but choosing how to respond to what we can't control. It can feel repetitive and stays surface-level, but as an approachable entry point into Stoic philosophy, it does exactly what it sets out to do.

Ryan Holiday·August 2025

During and after the Covid-19 pandemic I began to take a deeper interest in philosophy. Like many people of my generation I was trying to make sense of the difficult realities around me such as wars, the pandemic itself, and economic hardship. It is now 2025 and I believe we can all agree that wherever we live, the constant access to information and the way news outlets prefer to broadcast negative stories because they generate more views often leaves us drained. This endless stream of bad news, sometimes called disaster porn, creates a kind of compassion fatigue that can lead to depression and the feeling that we are the only ones carrying such a burden.

It was in this period that I discovered The Daily Stoic and Ryan Holiday’s Stoic philosophy channel on YouTube. At first I was prejudiced against Stoicism. I assumed it was a way of life for people who were emotionless and cold, like logical machines. When Ryan Holiday began talking about figures from ancient Rome I was skeptical. I wondered how the ideas of people who lived two thousand years ago could possibly apply to someone like me living in 2025, constantly online and overwhelmed with information.

This book, together with Marcus Aurelius’ Meditations (which I will review separately later), quickly proved me wrong about Stoicism. I was surprised by how closely the ideas in The Daily Stoic spoke to the very struggles I was facing in my own life. The wisdom felt timeless and unexpectedly relevant. Another strength of this book is its accessibility. The format is simple and inviting. It offers one page of wisdom for each day of the year, drawn from the writings of the Stoics and explained in plain, modern language. You can follow it as intended, reading one page each morning or night to carry with you throughout the day. Or, as I did, you can read large sections over a few months and still gain just as much from it.

Even if you are a slow reader or someone who gets distracted easily, this book remains very accessible. Each meditation is short enough to keep your attention, yet meaningful enough to leave you with something to reflect on. The daily entries explore themes such as self-control, resilience in the face of adversity, the importance of virtue, and the search for clarity in the middle of chaos. The commentary that accompanies the ancient Stoic texts feels modern and practical without ever being reduced to clichés. This balance makes the book especially valuable for readers who are new to philosophy and might otherwise find it intimidating.

What I liked most about this book is the way it emphasizes that stability and the ability to deal with calamities depend on our choices. We cannot control the major events happening around us, but we can always choose how to respond to them. This simple yet profound lesson changed the way I viewed Stoicism. I had once thought of Stoics as emotionless robots, but this book showed me otherwise. Marcus Aurelius himself endured the death of many of his children, faced a pandemic during his reign, and lived through wars. He did not have the luxury of ignoring his responsibilities, nor did he escape sadness, grief, or anger. The difference was that he refused to let those emotions take control of his actions. Instead, he adapted and chose to act in ways that served both him and his people. That is what Stoicism teaches us to do. It is not about suppressing feelings or becoming unfeeling, but about learning to adapt and to act with reason even in the hardest circumstances. There is a fine line between being cold and being resilient, and this book makes that distinction clear.

It also taught me that I should work on finding happiness in things that cannot be taken away from me. In today’s world many of us tie our sense of happiness to the things we can buy or access with money. The problem is that when money becomes scarce, so does our ability to feel content. This book reminded me that we should not attach our well-being to things that can be easily taken from us, or to pleasures that only give us a short-lived rush of dopamine. True stability comes from anchoring our happiness in values, choices, and practices that are not dependent on wealth or circumstance.

Is this book perfect? No. At times it repeats itself. The message is often the same, even if the quotation and the commentary that follow are different. That can be a little frustrating, especially if you read large sections at once instead of one page a day. The reflections also stay at a surface level rather than offering deep philosophical analysis. If you are already familiar with Stoicism or have read the original texts in depth, you may not find it very challenging. Still, as a tool for daily practice, the brevity and structure are exactly what make it effective.

That being said, this book is a great way to get into Stoicism. It may not be the most detailed or deeply analytical work, but it does exactly what it sets out to do: introduce timeless wisdom in a form that is accessible and practical. Whether you follow it day by day or read it in larger sections, it offers steady guidance that can help you face modern challenges with more clarity and resilience. I would especially recommend it to readers who are curious about Stoicism but feel intimidated by the original texts.

My rating is 4.5 out of 5 stars.
