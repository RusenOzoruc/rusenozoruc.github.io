The Rise of the Dragon: An Illustrated History of the Targaryen Dynasty, Volume One

by George R.R. Martin , Elio M. García Jr. , Linda Antonsson
★★★★★

The Rise of the Dragon offers a streamlined, richly illustrated take on Targaryen history from Aegon's Conquest through the Dance of the Dragons, trading Fire & Blood's dense detail for stunning, consistent artwork. A must-read for Targaryen and House of the Dragon fans, even if The World of Ice & Fire remains the better entry point for newcomers.

George R.R. Martin , Elio M. García Jr. , Linda Antonsson·January 2026

This is yet another history book from A Song of Ice and Fire by George R. R. Martin. The closest books I can compare this to are The World of Ice & Fire and Fire & Blood. However, unlike The World of Ice & Fire, which covers the entire history of the A Song of Ice and Fire universe, this book focuses exclusively on the history and reigns of House Targaryen.

The book covers Targaryen history starting with Aegon’s Conquest and continues up until the end of Aegon III’s regency following the Dance of the Dragons. In that sense, its scope is much narrower and more focused than some of the other companion books in the series.

Unlike Fire & Blood, which goes into extensive detail and reads like a dense fictional history, The Rise of the Dragon is essentially an illustrated version of that story. While I am not entirely certain, Fire & Blood feels significantly more text-heavy and detailed, whereas this book presents a more streamlined, almost bare-bones version of Targaryen history in order to make room for its illustrations.

That trade-off is largely worth it, because the illustrations are top-notch. The artwork throughout the book is visually striking and generally very consistent. I do, however, have one major complaint: I absolutely disliked the depiction of Dragonstone in this book. The version shown in The World of Ice & Fire was far superior in my opinion, both in terms of atmosphere and overall design.

Aside from that specific issue, the visual consistency is excellent, which is extremely important in visual storytelling and worldbuilding. Even when certain artistic interpretations did not fully work for me, the book maintains a coherent visual identity that strengthens its sense of being a genuine historical record of Westeros.

If you are interested in the history of the Targaryens, this is absolutely your book. Interest in House Targaryen has clearly increased following HBO’s House of the Dragon, and this book fits perfectly into that renewed attention. If you enjoyed the TV series, you are very likely going to love this book as well, especially since it presents a more coherent and better-written version of the same historical events.

As a standalone entry point into the A Song of Ice and Fire fandom, this book can work. That said, I would still recommend The World of Ice & Fire instead if you are looking for a broader and more comprehensive introduction to the world. The Rise of the Dragon is more niche by comparison, as it focuses solely on the Targaryens rather than the wider history of Westeros.

My rating for this book is 5 out of 5. It is an excellent read even if you have already read Fire & Blood. The illustrations, presentation, and focused approach make it absolutely worth both your time and your money.

By Ruşen M. Özoruç
