The Strange Demise of Titus Endor

by Dan Abnett
★★★★☆

A quiet, unsettling character study that pulls Titus Endor out of Eisenhorn's shadow, weaving in dementia, old ghosts, and a creeping sense of dread that feels earned rather than forced. A niche entry for series fans, but a genuinely compelling one. 4/5 stars

Dan Abnett·June 2026

The Strange Demise of Titus Endor is a short story from the Eisenhorn archives that doesn't actually feature Eisenhorn himself, instead it follows his old contemporary, Titus Endor, a man who always believed he was destined for greatness but instead finds himself chasing an elusive heretic at the backwater edge of the galaxy. It's a strange choice for a story, focusing on a side character rather than the main cast, but it works far better than expected.

What stood out to me most is the tone. This is a quiet, slow-burning, almost procedural story, Endor spends most of it alone, following clues, chasing a quarry who always seems just out of reach, and reflecting on his old master and on Eisenhorn, wondering what became of his old friend. Dan Abnett does a great job of weaving in just enough strange details and unsettling omens to make you feel like something is deeply wrong long before the story confirms it. It's genuinely creepy in a way that feels earned rather than forced.

Character-wise, this gave me a much better understanding of Titus Endor as his own person, rather than just Eisenhorn's old colleague. His mindset, his frustrations, his sense of having been passed over for greatness, all of it makes him feel human, and it makes his eventual demise land with real weight.

If I have one complaint, it's that this story lives very much on the fringes of the wider Eisenhorn series. If you're not already invested in this universe or these characters, some of the emotional weight might not fully land, this isn't really a jumping-on point. It's also a short story, so don't expect a fully fleshed-out arc, it's more of a mood piece than a plot-heavy tale.

Still, for what it is, a bleak, atmospheric character study, it succeeds. 4 out of 5 stars.
