Diablo Bestiary - The Book of Adria

by Robert Brooks , Matt Burns
★★★★☆

Book of Adria trades lore for gorgeous creature-design breakdowns of Diablo III's demons, making it more of an art-and-inspiration book than a narrative-heavy read. Light on text compared to other Character Tomes, but a great pick for anyone who loves Sanctuary's monster design.

Robert Brooks , Matt Burns·February 2026

This is the third companion book for the Diablo franchise, and it specifically focuses on Diablo III. While the first two books told us about the history of Sanctuary, Book of Lorath focused on artifacts, and Book of Prava focused on the aftermath of the Diablo IV story, this one is all about the creatures of Sanctuary. It mainly covers the main types of demons and foes from the games, everything from the common rank-and-file enemies you cut through by the dozen to the more iconic named bosses that stick in your memory long after you've beaten them.

What I appreciated most is how usable this book feels beyond just being a fan collectible. It's a great book to draw inspiration from for your own creative work, genuinely gets those creative juices flowing, whether you're into worldbuilding, tabletop monster design, or just want a deeper appreciation for how Blizzard's art team approaches creature design. Seeing the breakdowns of how these demons are conceptually built, their motifs, their thematic ties to sin and corruption, gives you a much better sense of the intentionality behind Sanctuary's roster of monsters than you'd get just from fighting them in-game.

We also get a bit of lore sprinkled in through tidbits from Adria and her motivations, which was a nice touch and ties the bestiary format back into the wider narrative instead of feeling like a completely disconnected encyclopedia entry.

The art itself is absolutely gorgeous, in usual Blizzard fashion, full of the same moody, grotesque detail that makes their creature designs so iconic. That said, it is light on written content compared to the other books in the series. Book of Prava, Book of Cain, and Book of Tyrael all had noticeably more text and narrative depth than this one. It probably edges out Book of Lorath slightly in terms of content, but it's still very much an art-forward entry rather than a lore-dense one.

Overall, this is a strong pick if you love Diablo's creature design and want something to flip through for inspiration or nostalgia, but if you're going in expecting a narrative-heavy read like some of the other Character Tomes, temper your expectations a bit.

4 out of 5 stars.
