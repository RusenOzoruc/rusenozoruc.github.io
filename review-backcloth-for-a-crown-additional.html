Backcloth for a Crown Additional

by Dan Abnett
★★★☆☆

A cozy, low-stakes detour between Malleus and Hereticus, where the real highlight is watching Eisenhorn and Bequin's relationship shine in a quieter, more personal setting. Enjoyable, but it adds little to the wider story

Dan Abnett·June 2026

Backcloth for a Crown Additional is a short story set between Malleus and Hereticus, and compared to the trilogy proper, it's a much more self-contained story. The death of an old friend, Lord Aen Froigre, pulls Eisenhorn and Bequin away from a rare moment of peace, and what starts as a personal, emotional investigation quickly turns into something stranger once traces of warpcraft turn up on the estate.

It was an easy, enjoyable read, low-key in a way the main novels rarely are, more paranormal detective mystery than the sprawling conspiracies and moral unraveling we've come to expect from Eisenhorn's bigger adventures. There's something nice about spending time with these characters in a smaller, quieter setting for once.

What bothered me, though, is that this story carries absolutely no stakes for the main narrative, at least not that I picked up on. Unlike Missing in Action, which quietly deepened Eisenhorn as a character, or even Titus Endor, which added real emotional weight to a side character, this one felt more like a fun detour than something that actually adds to the larger story. Nothing here seems to matter once you close the book and move on to Hereticus.

That said, the relationship between Bequin and Eisenhorn is easily the best part of this story. Getting to see them in a more relaxed, personal dynamic, away from the usual life-or-death stakes, was genuinely charming, and it's the one thing that made this worth reading beyond just completionist curiosity.

As a small, standalone piece of the wider Eisenhorn saga, it's a pleasant enough read, but not an essential one.

3 out of 5 stars.
