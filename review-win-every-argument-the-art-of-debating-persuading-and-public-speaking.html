Win Every Argument: The Art of Debating, Persuading, and Public Speaking

by Mehdi Hasan
★★★★☆

Mehdi Hasan turns his own viral debate and interview moments into a masterclass, breaking down persuasion into a learnable process built on evidence, structure, and emotional control rather than natural talent. Eloquent, easy to read, and genuinely useful, a must-read for anyone who loves to argue.

Mehdi Hasan·March 2026

Normally, I don't enjoy reading non-fiction books all that much, I usually see them as a chore I have to get through before I'm allowed to read fiction. That wasn't the case at all with this book. First, I have to say I don't agree with Mehdi Hasan on all political issues or his worldview, but I admire him as a person, and we're all allowed to disagree on things. That said, no one can deny his ability to debate and argue. I've also seen some reviews claiming Hasan somehow pushes his own political agenda throughout the book, and I find that baffling, at no point in any chapter did I feel that way. I suspect the people writing those reviews might just dislike him because of his views and came in with a negative bias already, so there's that. I've been following Mr. Hasan for a long time, I've watched almost all of his Oxford Union debates and his interviews, where he argues and debates masterfully, and with receipts, I might add. So when I discovered he'd written a book about arguing, as someone who was in debate club, studied poli sci, and has been involved in debate and generally enjoys it, I was stoked to read this book.

This is a masterfully written guide for anyone passionate about arguing and debating. Hasan lays the groundwork brilliantly, every chapter stays relevant to the topic, and each one is backed by great examples pulled from his own debates and interviews. He builds the book around a clear, structured process rather than vague tips, weaving in classical rhetoric, Aristotle's ethos, pathos, and logos show up throughout, alongside his own decades of experience as an interviewer and journalist. This also basically turns the book into a kind of live commentary. Hasan is known for viral moments from his debates and interviews, and this commentary gives real insight into those moments. Obviously, you need a bit of natural talent to become a great debater, but this commentary also underlines just how much work went into those viral, gotcha moments. It's fascinating to see the work behind the scenes, the preparation, the anticipation of counterarguments, the way a single sharp question gets built long before it's ever asked out loud.

The book also frames arguments as something built on evidence rather than feeling, and Hasan stresses how important it is to control your emotions while debating. Good arguments don't just appear, you have to do the research, find the evidence, and pay attention to structure and audience. Simply being right about a topic isn't enough, you have to convince people, back up your arguments with evidence, documentation, and sources, and stay consistent throughout. What I appreciated most is that none of this is treated as some kind of natural gift you either have or don't, Hasan breaks it down into a process anyone can actually learn and practice, step by step. This book walks you through all of that, and exactly what you need to do to get there.

Beyond his own debates, Hasan also draws on examples from history, debates that changed the trajectory of a nation, and gives us real insight into those moments as well. Figures like Churchill, Cicero, Demosthenes, and Martin Luther King all make appearances, and Hasan does a great job of connecting centuries-old rhetorical techniques to the very modern, very online arguments we find ourselves having today. It gives the book a nice balance, it never feels like it's just about winning Twitter fights, it's rooted in a much older tradition of public persuasion.

There are some negatives, though. As far as I remember, since I'm writing this review months after finishing the book, Hasan only gives examples from his winning moments. I've personally never seen him lose a debate or an argument, but I'm sure it's happened, he just never talks about those. It might have been valuable to get insight into those moments too, to learn about the mistakes he made and how he reflected on them afterward, since failure often teaches more than success does. Also, if you're picking this book up expecting a memoir of Hasan's life, you'll be disappointed, this is a much more technical book, focused squarely on technique rather than his personal journey.

To summarize, this book is a must-read if you want to learn how to debate and argue. It's eloquently written and easy to read. I recommend this book 100 percent, 5 out of 5 stars.
