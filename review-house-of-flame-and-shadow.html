House of Flame and Shadow

by Sarah J. Maas
★★★☆☆

"House of Flame and Shadow delivers a genuinely stunning multiverse twist connecting Crescent City to the ACOTAR world, but Bryce and Hunt's arcs, and their relationship, take a frustrating step backward while side characters Ruhn and Lidia steal the show. The rushed, too-convenient ending makes this the weakest of the trilogy, but the ambitious world-building still makes it worth the read

Sarah J. Maas·September 2025

This is the third book in the Crescent City series. In my opinion, out of the three, this one feels like the weakest, and in this review I’ll explain why. Spoiler warning: from this point on, I’ll be discussing major plot points from all three books.

The first book had the vibe of a murder mystery. The second mixed murder mystery with revolution. By the third book, the story fully shifts into an uprising and revolution. I think Sarah J. Maas handles these transitions between genres really well, and the shift itself is not where my problems lie. My main issue is that the story could have been divided into two separate books to allow for more detail. Some of the character arcs, especially for the main characters, also felt weak and frustrating.

Book two ended with our characters at their lowest point. Bryce was transported to another world, Hunt, Baxian and Ruhn were captured, and Lidia was left in the impossible position of pretending to serve the Asteri while secretly aiding the uprising and trying to protect Hunt, Ruhn and Baxian. Book three jumps into the action right away, and the pacing issues that dragged down the first book were handled much better here. The opening is engaging and easy to dive into.

The big twist is of course the crossover. Since Crescent City was the first Sarah J. Maas series I read, I didn’t realize the impact of seeing Nesta, Azriel, and Cassian when they appeared. At first, I didn’t even know who they were until other readers explained it was a big deal. Now that I’ve started the ACOTAR series and finished the first two books, I understand the significance. That is also why I waited before writing this review. And I have to say, the twist was superb. Learning that the Fae of Crescent City originated from the ACOTAR world stunned me. It was handled really well, and one of the things I respect about Maas is that she never resorts to info-dumping. The world-building is revealed through the eyes of the characters, and it feels natural and masterful.

Now onto the characters. Bryce’s arc is one of my biggest disappointments. In the first book she was resilient, overcoming her trauma and PTSD, and willing to do the right thing despite impossible odds. In the second book I already felt her character drift away from that, but in this book, especially in the first half, she becomes almost unbearable. She falls into this “I know best” mindset that makes her reckless and frustrating to follow. You want to root for her, but she comes across as self-absorbed. By the end she returns more to her old self, but the inconsistency weakens her arc.

Hunt was always a badass character in my eyes, but in this book he feels reduced to Bryce’s sidekick. He is hesitant to act, and although his trauma from Shahar’s rebellion and his torture under the Asteri palace make this understandable, it still feels like a step backwards. In the first two books he fought through his scars and found strength. Here, he often becomes so easily triggered that he feels sidelined and at times almost useless.

Their relationship, which was one of the best things about the first two books, suffers the most here. What started as a slow-burn romance that grew naturally ends up feeling wrong. Their reunion is underwhelming, and Bryce comes across as dismissive of Hunt’s feelings and trauma, which is completely out of character for her. Hunt seems like he is walking on eggshells the entire time. The relationship feels unbalanced, as if it is meant to make Bryce look strong while weakening Hunt, and it does more harm than good.

Ruhn and Lidia, on the other hand, completely shine in this book. They go through similar struggles, but unlike Bryce and Hunt, they handle them with patience and growth. Watching Lidia slowly open up to Ruhn and seeing them work through the challenges they face together was one of the highlights of the book. They became my favorite characters, and in my opinion, the MVPs of this installment. The same goes for Ithan and Tharion, who also had strong arcs that kept me invested. In fact, the side characters are stronger than the main ones in this book, and that shift in focus made me enjoy their chapters more than Bryce and Hunt’s.

As for the story itself, the ending felt rushed. The entire plot could have been stretched into two books, which would have allowed for better pacing and more detail. Instead, every problem had a solution that felt too convenient. Most side stories and even the main storyline wrapped up too quickly. The ending was predictable and underwhelming. Sarah J. Maas could have done much more with it, especially after the multiverse twist, but instead it all felt too easy. At no point did I believe the main characters were in any real danger. The one part that did surprise me was the reveal about Hunt’s father, which I thought added depth to his character. Still, the final act as a whole left me disappointed.

On the positive side, the lore and world-building remain as strong as ever. Maas has never struggled with building immersive, believable worlds, and this book proves it again. The introduction of the multiverse is not just exciting but also changes how I think about her other books and upcoming stories. Even so, the emotional scenes here did not hit me as hard as they did in the first two books.

Looking at the series as a whole, it was absolutely worth it. Book two is still the strongest and most powerful of the trilogy, and book one surprised me by pulling me into a genre I never thought I’d enjoy. Book three is the weakest, but still a good read. The side characters, world-building, and multiverse twist kept me engaged even when the main arcs fell flat.

I give this book 3 out of 5. It is the weakest of the series, but it is still worth reading. I recommend committing to the trilogy, because despite its flaws, it delivers an ambitious story and expands Sarah J. Maas’s universe in exciting new ways.

By Ruşen M. Özoruç
