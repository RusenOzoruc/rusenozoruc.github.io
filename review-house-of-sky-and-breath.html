Book Review
Romantasy

House of Sky and Breath

by Sarah J. Maas
★★★★☆

House of Sky and Breath somehow surpasses its explosive predecessor, diving straight into chaos from the prologue and never letting up through a jaw-dropping ending. With flawless worldbuilding, deepening romance between Bryce and Hunt, and standout character growth (especially Tharion), this is a rare 5-star sequel that delivers on every front.

Sarah J. Maas·August 2025

After the explosive ending of House of Earth and Blood, Sarah J. Maas returns with a sequel that raises the stakes, deepens the world, and drops a twist no one saw coming.

Oh boy, oh boy. I honestly thought this series had peaked with book one—that nothing could top the emotional weight and intensity of it. But wow… I was so wrong. House of Sky and Breath doesn’t just match the first but it surpasses it.

Unlike the slow build-up of Book 1, this one dives straight into the action and it never lets up. Right from the prologue, you’re thrown into chaos, tension, and revelations. It sets the tone for what’s coming, and from that point on, I was hooked. There’s barely a moment to breathe, and honestly? I loved every second of it.

The worldbuilding? Still flawless. Just like the first book, Maas manages to expand the universe without ever falling into boring info-dumps, even during chapters where the main characters are hit with shocking discoveries. It all feels natural and immersive, like we’re uncovering secrets right alongside them.

The romance continues to build in the background, and honestly? The chemistry between Bryce and Hunt is chef’s kiss. Their dynamic evolves in a really satisfying way. More emotional depth. They actually talk. They support each other. It’s slow-burn in the best way, and it never takes away from the main plot flows with it.

Just like in the first book, the final third of House of Sky and Breath is absolutely packed with action. Once it kicks off, there’s no slowing down. You physically can’t put the book down. Every chapter ends on a cliffhanger, every reveal hits harder than the last. Breaks? Not possible. Sleep? Overrated.

As for the side plots, I have mixed feelings about Ruhn and Agent Daybright. Their relationship felt a little rushed at times, but I won’t lie, they do have chemistry. There’s definitely potential there, and I’m curious to see how it develops in the next book.

Another thing I loved? The character development. All the side characters we got to know better in this book are so well written. Each of them has their own struggles, secrets, and moments of growth. Tharion especially stood out this time. His arc was messy, emotional, and honestly, kind of heartbreaking at times. I didn’t expect to care that much, but here we are.

The pacing in this book is another big win. It’s consistent, smooth, and never drags. It reads surprisingly easily. Especially for a chunky fantasy novel and compared to the first book, it’s a huge improvement in terms of flow.

The supporting cast, both new faces and returning ones, really pull their weight. Everyone has a role, and it all comes together to create a great, dynamic ensemble that adds emotional and plot depth.

Now, I do have a couple of small gripes. These are definitely personal pet peeves, so they might not bother everyone, but they stuck out for me.

First, compared to Book 1, Bryce almost feels too badass at times. One of the things I loved most about her in the first book was her struggle, her vulnerability, her grief, her slow growth into strength. Here, she’s a little too perfect in some scenes. Still fun to read, but I missed some of that rawness.

Another thing that pulled me out of the story was the way characters (especially the Vanir) seem to recover way too quickly from massive injuries. I know they're magical beings, but when everyone walks off near-death experiences like it’s nothing, it cheapens the stakes for me. Huge injuries should mean something and they should last, or at least have consequences. Just my take!

Also, while House of Earth and Blood leaned heavily into the murder mystery vibe, House of Sky and Breath shifts gears. There’s still a bit of mystery sprinkled in, but as the story unfolds, it becomes something much bigger, a tale of rebellion, hidden agendas, and rising revolution. And that shift? It works. It feels like the world is expanding right alongside the stakes.

And don’t even get me started on that ending. (No spoilers, don’t worry.) Let’s just say my jaw hit the floor.

If you loved Book 1, buckle up. This one’s even wilder. Sarah J. Maas really said “you thought that was intense? Just wait.”

This book is a rare 5-star read for me—absolutely delivered on every front

By Ruşen M. Özoruç
