Book Review
Romantasy

A Court of Frost and Starlight

by Sarah J. Maas
★★★☆☆

Less a full installment than a warm, plot-light bridge between books three and five, this novella lets the characters heal and breathe after the war while quietly setting up Nesta's arc. The multi-POV approach is a welcome shift, but the content is thin enough that it feels more like an extended prologue than a standalone story.

Sarah J. Maas·December 2025

Even though this book is labeled as 3.5, it is essentially the fourth book of the series. The most important thing to understand before reading it is its purpose. This is not a full installment with a strong plot. It is a connecting piece that exists between the third book and the fifth. It works as a cooldown after the war and sets up the emotional and narrative groundwork for what comes next.

There is not much plot here. It is more of a feel good story that lets the characters and the reader breathe after everything that happened in A Court of Wings and Ruin. We closed a major chapter in book three, and this novella shows how the characters deal with the aftermath. It focuses more on healing, day to day life, and small personal conflicts. In that sense, it is a calm break in the middle of the series, not an action heavy continuation.

One thing I genuinely liked was the use of multiple points of view. I usually criticize first person books because they limit how much we can understand other characters. This novella finally tackles that issue. Getting more than one point of view is a huge improvement in my opinion, and it felt refreshing compared to the earlier books.

The story also begins to shift the focus away from Rhys and Feyre and slowly prepares us for Nesta’s arc. The transition is not sudden, but it is clear, and you can feel the direction the series is taking. Because of that, I do think this book is necessary before the fifth one. Even though the plot is light, it sets up the next major storyline and fills in emotional details that would feel missing if you skipped it.

My main criticism is that this book could have easily been the first part of the next book or even a very long prologue. It does not stand strongly on its own. It serves a purpose, but the content is thin, and as a separate installment it feels unnecessary. That is the main reason my rating is 3 out of 5.

It is still enjoyable for what it is. It is warm, personal, and easy to read. But it is not a full story. It is a bridge. And while it does its job, it simply does not offer enough to score higher for me.

By Ruşen M. Özoruç
