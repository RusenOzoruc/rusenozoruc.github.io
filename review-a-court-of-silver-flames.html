A ​Court of Silver Flames

by Sarah J. Maas
★★★★☆

Silver Flames finally breaks from Feyre's first-person perspective, centering Nesta's brutal healing journey alongside standout new characters Gwyn and Emerie. Her romance with Cassian is beautifully built, and the Illyrian Rite trial outshines even the series' war scenes, making this one of the strongest entries yet

Sarah J. Maas·November 2025

This is the fifth book in the series, even if Goodreads lists it as the fourth. I consider Frost and Starlight to be the real fourth book because it acts as a bridge between the third and this one. That short novella shifts the tone, closes the first major arc with the defeat of King Hybern and the rogue human rulers, and hints that their lingering influence might become a problem later. In this book that possibility finally becomes real and the story enters a new stage.

What I liked most right away is that we are finally done with the first person perspective. I enjoyed this change immensely. The main characters are no longer Feyre and Rhys. This book belongs to Nesta, Cassian, and the Valkyries. It is a big shift and it is a risky one, because Nesta is a very divisive character in the fandom. Some people love her, many people cannot stand her. Before this book I was indifferent to her. I did not love her or hate her, and she was not a character I cared deeply about. Silver Flames changed that.

We see Nesta at absolute rock bottom. She is angry, isolated, and has burned almost all goodwill around her. A large part of the story is her healing journey. Feyre and Rhys even take measures to force her into a place where she has no choice but to face herself. It quickly becomes clear that Nesta is the most damaged of the three sisters. Her guilt, shame, and PTSD run deeper than anything Feyre or Elain ever dealt with. This book finally lets us see how heavy her burden is and how much she has been hiding.

The new side characters, Gwyn and Emerie, are very well written. Emerie technically appeared first in Frost and Starlight, and it is nice to see that her scene with Cassian was not pointless. Both of them feel original and distinct, and they play an important role in Nesta’s transformation. However, this highlights something I have noticed in both the ACOTAR series and the Crescent City series. Sarah J. Maas writes almost every character with a traumatic past. Their trauma becomes the explanation for every personality trait. It works sometimes, but it is also an easy shortcut. Not everyone needs a tragic past to be interesting. Some people can be good simply because they want to be good. Some people can be terrible simply because they enjoy it. Your past does not have to define your future.

Still, in this particular book, the trauma focus actually makes sense. Nesta, Gwyn, and Emerie all have painful histories and the bond they form is built on shared experience. Their connection feels real because they grow through their healing. Together they find strength and become better versions of themselves. In this story their trauma is not a cheap narrative tool but the foundation of their development.

In my review of the third ACOTAR book I mentioned how refreshing it was to see the loyalty of the Night Court inner circle. Here we get to see the other perspective. Nesta is on the outside looking in, and this gives us a completely different angle on a group that is usually presented as perfect. The book handles this shift very well and shows that even a supportive circle can feel suffocating when you are not part of it.

There is also Feyre’s pregnancy subplot. Many readers might feel tension here, but for me it did not work. As I have said in previous reviews, I never fear for the lives of the Fae in these books. Their regenerative abilities remove any real sense of danger. Even with the pregnancy complications, I never believed something bad would happen.

The relationship between Nesta and Cassian is written beautifully. Sarah J. Maas never fails when it comes to romance. What begins as lust grows slowly into real love. Their chemistry is strong, their bond believable, and their development steady. I genuinely enjoyed watching them grow closer.

I also enjoyed watching Nesta finally come into her own and find happiness with Gwyn and Emerie. Her relationship with the House was another pleasant surprise. The bond between Nesta and the House became a friendship I did not know I needed, and it ended up being my favorite relationship in the entire book.

Warning for spoilers. In the last third of the story Nesta and her Valkyries are forced into the Illyrian Rite, a brutal trial where they cannot use their powers and must survive to reach the top of the mountain. These scenes were written far better than the war scenes in the third book. The tension, pacing, and emotion were all stronger and more engaging.

Overall, this book is definitely one of the strongest in the series and my favorite after the second book. Nesta’s arc is powerful, the side characters are great, the romance is well executed, and the final act is gripping. My rating is 4 out of 5 stars.
