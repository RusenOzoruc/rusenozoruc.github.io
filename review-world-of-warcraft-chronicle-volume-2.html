World of Warcraft Chronicle: Volume 2

by Blizzard Entertainment
★★★★☆

Chronicle Volume 2 shifts from cosmic creation myths to grounded history, chronicling Draenor's orcish clans, their corruption, and the Horde's first invasion of Azeroth, with Gul'dan given especially rich treatment. Less conceptually groundbreaking than Volume 1, but essential for understanding the Horde's origins and identity.

Blizzard Entertainment·January 2026

This second volume of the World of Warcraft: Chronicle series focuses much more heavily on the history of Draenor. Draenor is one of the most important locations in the Warcraft universe, and this book treats it with the importance it deserves.

In many ways, this is fundamentally the Horde’s book. It covers the origins of the orcish clans, their culture, their corruption, and the events that ultimately lead to the Horde’s first invasion of Azeroth. The narrative follows this trajectory from Draenor to Azeroth and continues through the major conflicts, ending around the events of Warcraft III. Because of that, this volume is essential for understanding the Horde’s identity and historical context.

The book also goes further back and explores Draenor’s history long before the Horde’s invasion of Azeroth. It covers the Draenei after their escape from Argus and details how they settled on Draenor. In addition, the histories of the planet’s other major races and civilizations are explored, including the Botani, the ogres, and the Arakkoa. This broader perspective helps establish Draenor as a living world with its own deep and complex history, rather than just a backdrop for the orcs.

A large portion of the volume is dedicated to the origins, cultures, and internal conflicts of Draenor, and it places particular emphasis on one of the most influential characters in Warcraft history: Gul’dan. His role, motivations, and long-term impact on the universe are explored in far greater detail here, helping to contextualize many of the events that shape later stories in the franchise.

Just like the first Chronicle volume, you can clearly feel the passion behind this book. It was created at a time when Blizzard’s lore team was deeply invested in building a coherent and carefully structured world. The attention given to history, cultures, and cause-and-effect storytelling reflects deliberate worldbuilding rather than lore being written retroactively to support gameplay.

The artwork once again plays a major role in reinforcing that worldbuilding. It is detailed, atmospheric, and consistent, helping to visualize Draenor, its environments, and its people in a way that feels unified and intentional. As with Volume 1, the illustrations are not merely decorative; they actively support the lore and enhance the reader’s understanding of the setting.

From a worldbuilding perspective, this volume marks a clear shift away from purely cosmic concepts toward a more grounded historical focus. While Volume 1 concentrated on creation myths and universal forces, Volume 2 brings the spotlight closer to the cultures, civilizations, and political conflicts of the planet Draenor. This transition makes the world feel lived-in and historically layered, which is one of the Chronicle series’ greatest strengths.

Overall, Chronicle Volume 2 succeeds as a continuation of the foundation laid by the first book. It deepens the setting, strengthens the historical continuity of the universe, and gives weight to characters and conflicts that continue to echo throughout Warcraft lore. For readers interested in the internal history of the world rather than its cosmic origins, this volume represents a crucial step in the Chronicle series.

My final score for this book is 4 out of 5. It successfully builds on the foundation established in the first volume and shifts the focus toward a more grounded, historical form of worldbuilding. While not as conceptually groundbreaking as Volume 1, it remains a strong and important entry in the Chronicle series and is absolutely worth reading for anyone interested in Warcraft lore.
